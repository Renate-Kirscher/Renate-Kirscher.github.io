Six scones for Muster Hans!

An ingenious recipe
25. Oktober 2026

I am by far not as adventurous and outgoing as my mother, and all places I lived and worked at would not have enriched my character with the wonders of the host cultures, without her dragging me into them when she visited me. But there are places I am naturally drawn to which will remain an integral part of how I tell my stories about each place. The used-book store on Dupont Circle, the window-less meeting room in Ohio-University-physics-department-housing Clippinger hall, the used-book warehouse in Karlsruhe, a place off Jaffa street where the pricing was color-coded without any relation to page count and where I stumbled into a religious Jewish ceremony being held between the shelves, NY’s Strand, and a place in Manchester’s ungentrified parts, where one still felt the void and desolation the chimneys left behind.

In the latter, I spent time to find random gems at those random hours which were uncorrelated with those openings announced at the door. All the Cowper Powys et al. which I would never have indulged in, I found through a spooky attraction towards the upper shelves’ second rows. Anything that hard to reach and having been kept on-shelf for years of dust, is worth my time. I even broke my no-non-fiction rule when finding a compilation of a local student’s compilation of H. Bethe’s review articles. I heard and read about Bethe, but I was ignorant about his days in Manchester but this student seemed to have been influenced by him a lot given the heavily-annotated pages whose depth and insight made me take a second look on the name of the previous owner, just to realize I should not have identified the initials R.P. with those of a student.

I went home feeling elated with this stack of papers about which the two seemed to have discussed nature on a level I will always aspire to but never reach. On my way up Beetham Tower, something tangible fell out, and it is this, almost lost small slip of paper, which connects to a past even before the time when physics became a quantitative science: Why R.P. left this note is impossible to know, but not how he obtained it during his Cambridge days. But this I realized only a year later, when another fellow of some Cambridgian College used the exact same instructions that were jotted down.
